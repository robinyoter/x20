    NYC calculation & source

    Imported from the user-provided NYC calculation. The figures and its cited sources have not been independently verified for this city view.

    Method: national group wealth × NYC share of group households × an assumed wealth-intensity factor. Factors in the source: White 1.70, Black 1.10, Hispanic 0.75, Other 1.50. These reflect its housing, income and financial-wealth assumptions.

    The Asian split uses the source's extrapolation from 2022 SCF wealth relationships. Its uncertainty range is a scenario range, not a statistical confidence interval. This is a different model and period from the 2024 state estimates.

    NYC is not added to state or national totals: that would double-count a city already within New York State. Population, income and tax estimates have not been added to this city view.

    SOURCES & METHODOLOGY

    About the data

    The atlas uses the prepared 2024 dataset. The map and state rankings cover 50 states. The United States overview combines all 50 states and the District of Columbia, including group breakdowns.

    Population

    ACS 2024 1-Year, table B03002. Hispanic / Latino includes any race; all other groups exclude Hispanic people. State population totals come from ACS. In a few states, component estimates differ from the total by one person.

    Household income

    Aggregate annual household income in 2024 dollars. White non-Hispanic and Hispanic use B19025H/I. Other non-Hispanic groups are modeled using race-level income and population composition, calibrated to each state's income total. Household groups follow the householder's classification.

    Net worth

    All state and detailed group values are preliminary modeled estimates, not official state wealth statistics. Federal Reserve DFA national wealth at the end of 2024 is allocated using housing values, household counts and income, net of debt. DFA's Other category is split into five groups. Values are rounded to the nearest million dollars. National net worth includes DC and reconciles to the four DFA category totals.

    The model does not directly observe concentrations of stock ownership, private businesses or very wealthy families. ACS and DFA classifications are not identical. Reconciliation checks arithmetic, not accuracy.

    Federal income tax

    This is a mean-income model, not actual taxes paid. Mean household income equals aggregate income divided by household count. Each household is treated as one Single filer with a $14,600 standard deduction, ordinary-income tax brackets for 2024, and no tax credits. Tax on the mean income is multiplied by the number of households. State totals sum eight groups; the national total sums the state and DC calculations, rather than recalculating tax on a national mean.

    White non-Hispanic and Hispanic household counts come from ACS B25003H/I. Other groups use B25006 counts adjusted by non-Hispanic population shares. The model excludes itemized and additional deductions, preferential capital-gains rates, AMT, NIIT, payroll and self-employment taxes. A household may include multiple tax returns, and tax on mean income is not mean actual tax under progressive rates. Vermont's modeled zero Pacific Islander household count produces zero modeled tax.

    National scope and sorting

    National population and income are sums of state and DC values from the same dataset; modeled group results are aggregated consistently. They are not separately sourced national CPS estimates. State rankings use each state's overall totals and exclude DC. Click a group-table column heading to sort; click again to reverse direction.

    Reading the map

    Largest group means the most numerous group, even below 50%. In metric mode, color intensity is linear in the selected group's amount or share of the state total. Income and tax are annual flows; net worth is a year-end stock.
